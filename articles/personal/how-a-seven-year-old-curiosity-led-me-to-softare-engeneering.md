## How a 7-Year-Old's Curiosity Led Me to Software Engineering

This is a long story which takes me back to my primary school days. Since my primary school days, I was always a curious child who always wondered why?, how?, and "I want to know / I want to see how it works."

This curiosity led me to break things in the house such as radios, and other machinery such as mobile phones and hand watches (quartz/mechanical) because, as a child, I lacked the proper toolset and I used to borrow scissors and knives from the kitchen, which were not good enough to disassemble parts in electronics.

Fast forward to my college years, 11th and 12th, where I decided to take Management (Commerce) as my major since I had always avoided Mathematics during my primary school days because we used to have compulsory mathematics and optional mathematics, which is an alternative to economics, where I chose economics in my 10th, which led me to take management in my college.

However, during my 12th, I took the initiative and chose Business Mathematics instead of Business Studies as my optional subject, which introduced me to foundational linear algebra, calculus, stats & probabilities, and I worked hard but enjoyed it. This was my first step towards technology and the first spark towards quantitative fields, which later pulled me into computer science.

And at the time back in Nepal, kids only got their first mobile phone when they passed out of 10th and started college, and the same thing happened with me. I got my brand new smartphone, which was a Windows Lumia series, but before that, I also had one more Samsung device, which I had already bricked by trying to jailbreak it using the LuckyPatcher tool to install apps that were not available and supported on my device and use mods for Android games to get unlimited coins and gems in games. This was another catalyst which pushed me a bit closer and built that urge to know how to code and write programs, especially Android applications, so that I could extract the src files from the application binaries and hack the coins. Naive me back then, who had zero knowledge about how technology works, used to think that there must be some stored values that I could edit in the src code and then I would be good to go with unlimited game coins.

And fast forward to my first bachelor's degree, which I opted for in Business & Accounting, which was natural considering I did economics in 10th and Management (Commerce) in 11th and 12th.

In uni, I made another friend who always used to flex that he was a hacker by running a terminal in Kali Linux and some Python scripts. He was the final catalyst who drew me to finally get my hands on programming.

Therefore, after the first year, he dropped out and started learning programming and bootcamps, and after a few months into the 2nd year, I dropped out as well and joined a software development training program where my initial intention was again to learn to code Android apps and hack game coins in this online Pokémon game so that I could gain unlimited diamonds and upgrade my Pokémon cards and equip them with legendary items and face people in online PvP.

However, this program changed my trajectory from someone who wanted to learn to code apps just to hack coins to someone who now wants to build tools and apps that really help people in real life.

From that training, I spent 18 months learning programming fundamentals, OOP, basics of web development, and finally Mobile App development in the Flutter framework, with which I started my professional career as a Flutter Mobile Developer in Nepal during the COVID lockdown time, where I worked from home remotely with a startup based in Kathmandu.

While working as a mobile app developer, I stumbled upon several roadblocks as a developer who skipped the base fundamentals of computer science such as Operating Systems, Networking, and Data Structures and Algorithms and the mathematics for computer science, which made me pursue further study and a career in the UK. Then I arrived in the UK with zero experience in the industry and zero networks, and then during my first year, my perseverance and consistency led me to secure a volunteering frontend engineer role at a startup based in London, where I learned and developed frontend development and UI/UX skills using Figma, React.js, SCSS, and Next.js. Then, after that internship, I participated in multiple coding events and hackathons such as the G-Research coding challenge at the University of Oxford, where I met other brilliant people who were way better than me, which motivated me to work hard and catch up to them, and I attended HackSheffield9 at the University of Sheffield, where I met other people who were really passionate and put work towards what they love, and this was in my second year where I was constantly applying and preparing for placements and summer internships and attending events and networking. This experience and the insights I received from going beyond my circle gave me confidence and an overview of how to approach your passion and build a career out of it, which helped me to secure my university summer internship, where I worked as an IT Officer intern for a local GP partnered with the NHS to research and build a working medical triage prototype - a conversational voice bot for patient intake and symptoms assessment and triage, which I successfully delivered by the end of the 6-week long internship, where my supervisor, who was a GP, was so impressed that he kept in touch even after the internship on contributing to handing over the project to the new intern and knowledge transfer.

Whilst doing the internship, I managed to secure a placement offer from NEXT plc to work as a Software Engineer Placement in the Head Office based in Enderby, Leicester, where I worked in a Supply Chain department to maintain and deliver end-to-end features on sophisticated data-intensive logistics systems which move, track, and manage millions of items with precision per day. I developed a strong foundation in backend engineering along with frontend with C#/.NET, Azure, SQL Server, Windows Server & IIS, Linux server, Docker, and Azure DevOps (Azure CI/CD Pipelines). I also took full advantage of this placement opportunity to expand my horizons into security measures, troubleshooting in production, and provisioning infrastructure resources for setting up new server environments on-premises/cloud whilst in my placement.

My manager, lead engineer, and senior engineer were really impressed with my attitude towards owning job responsibilities and seeking help when needed after exhausting all possible solutions and attempts to solve problems at hand. My curiosity to know beyond and dissect how things work under the hood and in other components of a product and technology was praised by my manager and lead engineer. My communication skills and presentation were also praised by my colleagues within my team and in another team, to whom I had delivered that feature, who stated that the presentation was brilliant and clear after the feature delivery handover.

Likewise, I started the placement as a clean slate, and by the end of the placement, I was full of all the achievements I had accomplished throughout the placement.

I am not the smartest person in the room, but I work hard to keep up with those people in the room. I do not just want a career where I just do the bare minimum and it will be okay, which helps the business to operate and work as usual. I always strive for better and improvement and further development in terms of personal development and technical development in my career and personal life.

To simply put, how did I become interested in coding? It is because I have the mind of that seven year old kid who has a hunger for curiosity to know how?, what?, and why? about anything interesting he finds, and that interesting thing for me is the computer and the Internet: What is it, how information is transmitted from one computer to another, and how electrical currents are controlled and converted into bits, and how those bits are used to encode information, and how these bits are transmitted over several abstractions in a computer and travel through wires at light speed to connect with the world.
